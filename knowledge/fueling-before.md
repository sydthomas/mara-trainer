# Fueling before runs and the race

## Bottom line for the coach
- Before a long run or the race, plan a carbohydrate-based meal or snack a few hours out: the women's position stand says three to four hours before endurance exercise, and the dietitians' consumer guidance says one to four hours depending on what the runner tolerates. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- Don't eat right before the start; digestion competes with the working muscles and can upset the gut. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- Rehearse the pre-run meal and its timing on training long runs, never for the first time on race day. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- Carb loading is an option in the last three days before the marathon, but for women it only works when intake is set as an amount of carbohydrate, not as a bigger share of the plate, and evidence that it improves women's race performance is mixed. Point Sydney to a sports dietitian for amounts rather than computing them. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@acsm-and-dc-nutrition-athletic-performance-2016]
- Women report more stomach trouble in the early-follicular and late-luteal days of the cycle; suggest Sydney note cycle day alongside any pre-run GI issues. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Any sign of restriction, weight-loss pressure or guilt around food: be supportive, give no numbers, and refer to a doctor or registered dietitian (the joint position statement recommends an RDN, ideally a sports-dietetics specialist, for individual plans). [@acsm-and-dc-nutrition-athletic-performance-2016]

## What the sources agree on
- Eat carbohydrate in the hours before endurance exercise. The women's position stand advises a carbohydrate meal or snack three to four hours before, and a small amount (about 30 g) before sustained hard efforts of around an hour. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- The general sports recommendation is 1 to 4 g of carbohydrate per kg in the 1 to 4 hours before training or competition (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight). For women doing aerobic exercise the review suggests at least 1 g/kg in the hours before (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight), while noting this guidance rests mostly on data from men (for example, trained male cyclists whose thigh glycogen rose 42% after a pre-exercise carbohydrate meal four hours out). [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Pre-workout snacks should pair carbohydrate with a little protein; the dietitians' examples include a banana with peanut butter, Greek yogurt with berries, oatmeal with milk and fruit, or an apple with nut butter. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- Timing and food choices before exercise are individual and should be tested in training. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Carb loading: the review of female-specific strategies suggests women may load with 8 to 10 g of carbohydrate per kg per day over the three days before an event (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight), based on a study of trained endurance men and women that found loading worked in either cycle phase. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Both female-focused sources agree that women need loading targets expressed as an absolute carbohydrate amount; raising carbohydrate as a share of energy left trained female cyclists with no gain in glycogen or performance, while men in the same study improved, partly because the men ate more food overall. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Loading-level carbohydrate amounts can be hard for women with lower usual energy intakes to reach, because carbohydrate would have to make up most of what they eat. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]

## Where they differ
- Pre-run window: the women's position stand names three to four hours before endurance exercise; the female-strategies review and the dietitians' page use a wider one to four hours. The wider window reflects general (mostly male) guidance and individual tolerance. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021] [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- Carb loading by cycle phase: the position stand reports that loading at 8.4 to 9.0 g/kg/day (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight) raised resting glycogen in the mid-follicular phase (17 to 31% rises, versus 18 to 47% reported for men) but failed or gave only a modest 13% rise in the mid-luteal phase, and that higher follicular glycogen did not improve time-trial performance. The female-strategies review instead cites a luteal-phase study in trained women where a high-carbohydrate diet raised glycogen 13% more than a moderate one and improved submaximal endurance, and says a short luteal boost may store glycogen well when usual intake is lower. The studies differ in phase, baseline diet and whether the outcome was glycogen or performance. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Who loading helps: the female-strategies review presents loading as a reasonable option for women; the position stand is more cautious, stressing small, phase-dependent glycogen gains and the practical difficulty of eating enough. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]

## Women-specific notes
- In the luteal phase the body uses less carbohydrate and glycogen during exercise above about half of maximal aerobic capacity, and the position stand advises prioritizing carbohydrate in every phase, with more in the luteal phase and during active pill weeks for oral contraceptive users. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Women show slower gastric emptying and gut motility than men and more exercise-related stomach slowing, which argues for leaving enough time between the pre-run meal and the start. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Combined oral contraceptive use may hide early signs of low energy availability, so missed periods are not a reliable warning sign for pill users; refer any concern to a doctor. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Sex-specific loading data come from small studies; the position stand calls the evidence limited and phase-dependent. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]

## Gaps
- Fluids and sodium before running: see hydration.md. Everyday eating: see daily-nutrition.md. Caffeine before the race: see fueling-during.md.
- No approved source we could read gives race-morning specifics for the marathon (exact breakfast size, a pre-start top-up snack, or how to adjust for an early start time).
- No approved source we read lists specific foods to avoid before running (fat, fiber, protein amounts); the dietitians' page only advises against eating immediately before exercise.
- No source gives carb-loading guidance in absolute grams; all loading targets are per kg of body mass, which the coach does not use. A sports dietitian should set any loading plan.
- The ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada joint statement is paywalled; only its abstract was read, which has no numbers.
- ROAR (Sims, 2024) is approved but is a book cited by reference only; nothing from it is summarized here.
