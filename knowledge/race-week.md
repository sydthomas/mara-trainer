# Taper, race week and race day

## Bottom line for the coach
- Taper for 2 to 3 weeks. The pooled research favors tapers of 21 days or less, with the biggest benefit from 8 to 14 days. Published marathon plans use 2 weeks (Boston) or 3 weeks (Higdon, LA Road Runners). [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]
- Cut volume, not intensity. The meta-analysis found time-trial gains when training volume fell by 41 to 60% while intensity and session frequency stayed the same. Tapers that cut intensity showed no benefit. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- Keep a little race-pace or faster running through race week: short marathon-pace segments, a short threshold piece and strides (LA Road Runners), a 15-minute progression tempo (Chicago), a final short marathon-pace run (Boston). [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans]
- Race-week runs are short and easy, with rest or very light running in the last day or two. Higdon's novice race week is 3, 4 and 2 mi, then a rest day before the race. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan]
- On race day, pace by effort. Pace targets are ranges and shift with weather, elevation, fatigue and sleep. A first-timer can't know marathon pace from training alone. A recent half marathon is the usual way to estimate it. [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- Walking through aid stations is a legitimate strategy, and walk breaks work best taken by choice, early, before fatigue forces them. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- Plan easy recovery after the race: a few days of rest (Chicago), or a week of 30-minute walks (LA Road Runners). [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]
- The taper evidence comes almost entirely from trained men. Tell her so when it matters. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]

## What the sources agree on
- **Tapering works.** A meta-analysis of 14 studies (174 trained endurance athletes aged 17 to 32, mostly middle- to ultra-distance runners and cyclists) found that tapering improved time-trial performance (SMD -0.45) and time to exhaustion (SMD 1.28). VO2max and movement economy did not change. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Taper length.** All taper lengths of 21 days or less improved performance. The 8 to 14 day group had the largest effect (SMD -1.47, 95% CI -2.75 to -0.19), 15 to 21 days came next, and 22 days or more had the smallest effect. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Volume cut.** Only the 41 to 60% volume reduction band improved time trials significantly (SMD -0.77, 95% CI -1.23 to -0.30). Cuts of 20% or less, 21 to 40%, and 60% or more were not significant. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Keep intensity and frequency.** Keeping intensity helped (SMD -0.55, 95% CI -0.79 to -0.31). Lowering it did not (SMD 0.25, CI crossing zero). Keeping the number of sessions helped (SMD -0.53). Cutting frequency was not clearly beneficial (the paper's text is inconsistent on its significance, but its CI crosses zero). [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Shape of the taper.** Progressive (gradual) and step (sudden) tapers both improved performance, the progressive one slightly more (SMD -0.51 vs -0.38). Adding a short overload block before the taper beat a standard taper. The authors' summary is to taper for 21 days or less, cutting volume progressively by 41 to 60% without changing intensity or frequency. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Published plans taper by cutting volume gradually.** Higdon Novice 1 goes from a 40 mi peak week to 29, 21, then 9 mi plus the marathon, with long runs of 12 and 8 mi in the first two taper weeks. LA Road Runners Level 2 goes from a 20 mi long run four weeks out to 10 mi, then 6 mi, and labels the last three weeks "taper". Boston uses a 2-week taper with shorter sessions. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans]
- **Intensity stays in, in small doses.** In race week, LA Road Runners keep 20-minute marathon-pace runs, a 10-minute threshold piece with four 50 m strides, and 10 minutes at marathon pace the day before the race. Chicago's race-week speed session is a 15-minute progression from recovery pace up to best pace. Boston ends with a short marathon-pace run. [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans]
- **Back off in the days before.** Chicago advises easing off distance or pace a few days before any race. Higdon schedules rest the day before. [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- **Finding race pace.** Chicago builds every training pace from a race run in the last two months, using a chart that maps 5K, 10K and half-marathon times to marathon pace. Boston gives approximate per-mile paces for goal times from 2:45 to 5:00. Higdon recommends a pace calculator after the week-8 half marathon. LA Road Runners need a race or a 1 to 2 mile time trial to set paces. The coach uses `coach pace` from her saved race result and never invents a pace. [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]
- **On the day.** Chicago says pace targets are the middle of a range and effort is the better guide when conditions change. Higdon recommends walking through aid stations, since drinking is easier at a walk. [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- **Logistics for the LA Marathon.** The race starts at 7 a.m. LA Road Runners train at that hour and offer pace leaders for 3 to 7 hour finish times. Course details are in `course.md`. [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]
- **After the race.** Chicago advises a few days of rest and recovery. LA Road Runners schedule only 30-minute walks and days off in the week after. [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]

## Where they differ
- **Taper length: research versus plans.** The meta-analysis found the largest effect at 8 to 14 days. Higdon and LA Road Runners taper for 3 weeks, Boston for 2. Chicago keeps big sessions until three weeks out (a 99-minute long run and a 15K tempo), then drops to a 10 mi long run and race week. Why: the studies tested trained athletes, not first-time marathoners, and marathon plans hold extra time to recover from the last 18 to 20 mi run. All of them stay within the 21-day window the review supports. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@boston-marathon-baa-training-plans] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan]
- **How much to keep running in race week.** Every plan read here keeps a few short runs in race week, and the meta-analysis found that keeping session frequency during a taper helped. Higdon's novice race week is 9 mi against a 40 mi peak; the plan rules allow up to a quarter of the peak week before the race. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **How much intensity a beginner keeps.** Higdon's novice taper is all easy running. LA Road Runners even keep a 2-mile time trial two weeks out. Chicago keeps an interval session two weeks out. The meta-analysis supports keeping intensity, but it studied trained athletes, not first-timers. Why: Higdon's novice plan has no speed work to keep. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans] [@chicago-marathon-training-plan] [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- **Walking.** Higdon builds walking into his race strategy. The race-organizer plans read here say nothing about it, apart from LA Road Runners welcoming run/walkers at Level 1. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training] [@la-marathon-larr-training-plans]

## Women-specific notes
- The taper meta-analysis studied mostly male athletes with few women, had no sex subgroup, and its authors warn that the results may not represent female athletes. [@wang-tapering-endurance-meta-analysis-2023]
- That fits the wider pattern: across 5,261 sport-science papers, 34% of participants were women and 6% of papers studied women alone. [@cowley-invisible-sportswomen-2021]
- Studies of women should report cycle phase and contraceptive use, because hormones can change responses. The taper studies don't give this, so there's no evidence on whether women should taper differently. [@elliott-sale-methods-women-research-2021]
- If race day falls on her period: performance may be trivially lower in the early follicular phase (pooled effect -0.06, with a credible interval crossing zero; low-quality evidence). The authors advise against general rules and suggest she learn her own pattern in training. The race plan doesn't need to change because of cycle phase alone. [@mcnulty-menstrual-cycle-performance-2020]

## Gaps
- No approved source read covers even versus negative pacing splits, or how fast to start a first marathon. *Advanced Marathoning* (which covers race-day strategy) couldn't be read: its publisher page returned HTTP 429.
- No approved taper study is marathon-specific or studies novice or recreational runners. No approved source tests a taper in women.
- Race-morning routine (wake time, warm-up, bathroom, gear check, corral timing) isn't covered by any source read here. The London Marathon pages are marked not fetchable.
- *Daniels' Running Formula* (taper and race chapters) and Venice Run Club returned HTTP 429 and weren't read.
