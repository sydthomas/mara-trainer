# Fueling after runs

## Bottom line for the coach
- After long or hard runs, eat carbohydrate plus protein soon after finishing; the dietitians' guidance says within about an hour of an intense workout, and the women's position stand stresses eating carbohydrate quickly after prolonged exercise. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Easy recovery options from the dietitians include a milk-and-fruit smoothie, low-fat chocolate milk, a turkey and vegetable wrap, or yogurt with berries; drinks also help rehydrate. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance]
- For protein after a run, a practical absolute target is a 20 to 30 g serving, with protein spread across the day in similar servings rather than one large meal. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- When sessions are close together (a long run followed by another run the next morning), don't delay carbohydrate: in men, waiting two hours after exercise reduced glycogen storage. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- In the luteal phase, aim for the higher end of protein guidance after exercise. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Evidence on the best post-run protein dose for women is still thin; present amounts as guidance, not rules, and point to a sports dietitian for a personal plan. [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020] [@acsm-and-dc-nutrition-athletic-performance-2016]
- Any sign of restriction, weight-loss pressure or guilt around food: be supportive, skip numbers, refer to a doctor or registered dietitian. [@acsm-and-dc-nutrition-athletic-performance-2016]

## What the sources agree on
- Recovery eating should include both carbohydrate and protein. [@eatright-sports-athletic-performance] [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Carbohydrate should come soon after prolonged exercise: the women's position stand advises at least 1.2 g/kg quickly after prolonged exercise (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight); the female-strategies review advises at least 0.75 g/kg right after (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight), citing a follicular-phase study in which women stored glycogen as well as men. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Post-exercise protein for premenopausal women, including pill users: 0.32 to 0.38 g/kg as soon as possible after exercise (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight), with the upper end in the luteal phase. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- The IOC consensus gives a similar per-meal range of 0.3 to 0.5 g/kg (per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight) three to four times a day, with post-exercise intake consistently effective; protein supplements typically supply 20 to 50 g per serving. [@ioc-dietary-supplements-2018]
- In the systematic review of 14 studies (204 premenopausal women), protein within an hour after exercise appeared helpful, while protein before sleep had no or trivial effects on next-morning performance. [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020]
- In a study of women in the follicular phase, a recovery drink of carbohydrate with a little protein and fat after each of four weekly sessions improved later endurance; but a pre-sleep chocolate milk or about 40 g of casein did not improve next-morning running or recovery, though pre-sleep protein may reduce soreness in women. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Protein supplements and sports bars may contain other ingredients and carry a contamination risk; food first, and third-party certified products if supplements are used. [@ioc-dietary-supplements-2018] [@usada-supplement-connect]

## Where they differ
- Post-run carbohydrate amount: the women's position stand says at least 1.2 g/kg while the female-strategies review says at least 0.75 g/kg (both per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight). The higher figure is general guidance for fast refueling; the lower one comes from a single follicular-phase study where women matched men's glycogen storage. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Post-run protein dose: the women's position stand gives a specific range (0.32 to 0.38 g/kg), the IOC gives 0.3 to 0.5 g/kg, and the female-strategies review gives absolute 20 to 30 g servings (per kg ranges are per kg body mass — the coach doesn't ask for or compute with body weight). The systematic review of women concludes the best acute dose for female athletes is still undetermined; its only endurance study used a smaller mixed supplement, and male endurance studies suggest higher doses. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@ioc-dietary-supplements-2018] [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021] [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020]
- Cycle phase: the women's position stand advises more protein in the luteal phase because protein breakdown is higher then; the systematic review found too few, too confounded studies to say whether cycle phase or contraception changes needs, and one small study saw no phase difference in muscle protein building 24 hours after endurance exercise. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023] [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020]

## Women-specific notes
- Most direct evidence on women's protein needs comes from the systematic review of 14 studies in 204 recreationally active or competitive premenopausal women; studies were small, and cycle phase and energy intake were often not controlled. [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020]
- That review estimated daily protein needs for endurance-training women in the mid-to-upper part of sports guidelines; see daily-nutrition.md for daily totals. [@mercer-protein-female-athletes-2020]
- Women break down more protein at rest and after endurance exercise in the luteal phase, which is the basis for the luteal upper-end advice. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- In women, milk taken right after and one hour after resistance exercise improved strength and lean mass compared with a carbohydrate drink. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]
- Some low-androgen progestin pills reduced muscle protein building at rest and after endurance exercise in the studies the position stand reviewed; the evidence is limited. [@issn-female-athlete-nutrition-2023]
- Whey protein isolate may be easiest on the stomach for women, though no study data are given. [@wohlgemuth-female-nutrition-strategies-2021]

## Gaps
- Fluids and sodium after runs: see hydration.md. Daily protein and everyday eating: see daily-nutrition.md.
- No approved source gives an hourly carbohydrate refueling rate for a short (under about 8 hour) gap between two sessions; only the general advice to eat carbohydrate quickly.
- No approved source gives absolute post-run carbohydrate amounts in grams; all are per kg of body mass, which the coach does not use.
- The male delayed-carbohydrate study (two-hour delay) has no female replication in the sources read.
- The ACSM joint position statement is paywalled; only its abstract (no numbers) was read.
