# Course-specific preparation

## Bottom line for the coach
- LA Marathon: point-to-point 26.2-mile course nicknamed "Stadium to the Stars". It starts at Dodger Stadium at 7:00am and finishes at Santa Monica Blvd and Avenue of the Stars in Century City. The next edition listed is Sunday, March 7, 2027. [@la-marathon-course]
- LA time limit: 6 hours 30 minutes, counted from when the last runner crosses the start line. [@la-marathon-course]
- Because the race is point-to-point, the start and finish are far apart. The runner should plan how she gets to Dodger Stadium before the 7:00am start and how she gets home from Century City. No approved source gives shuttle or parking details. [@la-marathon-course]
- The organizer's LA course page doesn't list elevation, aid-station locations or what is served on course. For those, point the runner to the official course-map PDF linked from that page and to the organizer's race-week information. Don't fill in aid-station miles or hills from memory. [@la-marathon-course]
- For any other race, check the organizer's own course page first and quote only what it states (see the pointers below). [@la-marathon-course]

## What the sources agree on
- LA Marathon weekend runs March 5 to 7, 2027. It includes the LA 5K on Saturday, March 6, and the LA Charity Half Marathon on race day. The charity half also starts at Dodger Stadium (8:15am) and is about 13.7 miles, so it isn't an official half-marathon distance. [@la-marathon-course]
- The LA page says the route passes through many of the city's neighborhoods and landmarks but doesn't name them in its text. The route itself is on the course-map PDF. [@la-marathon-course]
- Boston: the B.A.A. publishes an official course map PDF and a media guide with an elevation profile. Neither could be read during the audit, so pull facts from them directly. [@boston-marathon-course-map-2026] [@boston-marathon-media-guide-2026]
- Chicago: a flat loop through 29 neighborhoods that starts and finishes in Grant Park. The next date listed is October 10, 2027. [@chicago-marathon-course]
- NYC: New York Road Runners' official course page has a route through all five boroughs (not fetched; the site blocks automated access). [@nyc-marathon-course]
- London: the official course page describes the route mile by mile (not fetched). [@london-marathon-course]
- Berlin: billed as the flattest of the World Marathon Majors, with very little elevation gain. The page lists 15 aid stations; drink-mix stations are at 9, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 36 km, and a gel station at 27.5 km. The map shown was still the 2026 route. [@berlin-marathon-course]
- Tokyo: runs from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building to the Wadakura Gate intersection. The next date listed is March 7, 2027, and the lead runner's expected start is 9:10. [@tokyo-marathon-course]
- Sydney: runs from Miller Street in North Sydney, over the Harbour Bridge, to the Sydney Opera House. It climbs 313 m in total with a net drop of 83 m. Mass start is at 6:30am, the cutoff is 7 hours, and the next date listed is August 29, 2027. [@sydney-marathon-course]

## Where they differ
- Terrain varies widely among the majors. Berlin and Chicago describe themselves as flat, while Sydney lists 313 m of climbing. Hill-specific preparation therefore depends on the race, and no approved source gives elevation figures for LA. [@berlin-marathon-course] [@chicago-marathon-course] [@sydney-marathon-course] [@la-marathon-course]
- Organizers publish different levels of detail. Berlin lists aid stations by kilometer and Sydney gives elevation, while the LA, Chicago and Tokyo page texts give neither in a readable form. [@berlin-marathon-course] [@sydney-marathon-course] [@la-marathon-course] [@chicago-marathon-course] [@tokyo-marathon-course]

## Women-specific notes
- No approved source addresses this. Course pages carry no sex-specific information. [@la-marathon-course]

## Gaps
- LA elevation profile, aid-station locations and contents (water, electrolyte drink, gels), medical tents, pacers, corrals or waves, bag check, and shuttle or parking logistics: none appear in the page text. The official course-map PDF was downloaded, but its labels couldn't be extracted. A human-readable look at the PDF, or the organizer's race-week guide, should be added as a source.
- LA race-day weather (typical March temperatures, heat contingencies): no approved source. See `hydration.md` for heat guidance.
- General course-preparation advice (training for hills or downhills, practicing on the course, rehearsing the start, pacing a point-to-point course, weather adjustments): no approved source covers it in this section; see `race-week.md` and `training.md`.
- Boston: the course map PDF couldn't be parsed and the media guide PDF was too large to fetch, so there are no Boston facts.
- London and NYC: marked not fetchable in the registry, so only pointers.
- Tokyo: its elevation profile is an image only and wasn't read. Berlin: elevation is listed as to be announced, and start and finish details are in a separate PDF that wasn't read.
