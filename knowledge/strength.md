# Strength training for runners

## Bottom line for the coach
- Adding strength work to running tends to help distance runners: in the main review, running economy improved by about 2 to 8% and time trials of 1.5 to 10 km tended to improve, with no harm to VO2max and almost never any gain in body mass. Most of that evidence is from men (about 79% male). [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Start with heavy, multi-joint, free-weight lower-body lifts (squats, deadlifts, step-ups, lunges); the review's authors suggest heavy work as the starting point and a mix with explosive and plyometric work later on. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- A building dose is 2 sessions a week of 2 to 3 hard sets per exercise; for maximal strength, general adult guidance points to loads of at least 80% of a one-rep max, but runners gained just as much with moderate loads (60 to 80%, 5 to 15 reps). Sets do not need to go to failure; stopping 2 to 3 reps short is enough. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Keep strength away from key runs: at least 24 hours between a lifting session and a hard running session, and more than 3 hours after hard running if both fall on the same day. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- In the racing phase, one session a week appeared to hold the gains; stopping completely let them fade back to baseline within about 6 weeks. The plan rules allow 2 strength days a week, and 1 in taper and race week. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- For women the evidence is thin but encouraging: trained women gained a lot of strength with no loss of endurance, and women in a collegiate in-season study likely raced slightly faster. One women-only trial found no change in running economy or 40-minute run performance, so promise strength and resilience, not a guaranteed faster marathon. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016] [@barnes-resistance-training-cross-country-2013]
- Runners new to lifting should start with 1 to 2 sets and get coaching on technique; studies without supervision showed no strength gains. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]

## What the sources agree on
- Why runners lift. Across 24 studies (469 runners aged about 17 to 45, mostly moderately to highly trained), strength training added to running for 6 to 20 weeks improved running economy by about 2 to 8%, most often at faster speeds (above about 75% of the speed at VO2max). Six studies found no economy gain, likely because they used machine-only or single-joint exercises or trained less often. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Performance. In that review, 8 of 12 time trials improved more than in controls: about 3 to 5% for middle-distance events and about 2 to 4% for events up to 10 km. Programs of 8 to 11 weeks gave bigger gains (about 3 to 5.5%) than 6-week programs (about 2.5%). Short sprint speed (20 to 30 m) improved by about 1 to 3.4% in every study that tested it, which matters for a finishing kick. No study tested a marathon. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Aerobic fitness is not harmed. VO2max was mostly unchanged, and blood lactate markers changed little. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] In 19 well-trained women, 11 weeks of heavy leg work left VO2max, lactate threshold speed and capillary density unchanged even though muscle fibers grew. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- Bulk is not a real risk at runner doses. Body mass did not change in 18 of the runner studies (one reported a 2% rise), and the strength gains were mostly neural. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Types of strength work:
  - Heavy resistance: typically 2 to 6 sets of 3 to 10 reps at more than 70% of one-rep max. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
  - Explosive resistance: light to moderate loads moved fast; general adult guidance for power is 30 to 70% of one-rep max with a low to moderate total of 24 reps or fewer per exercise, for example jump squats or Olympic-lift variations. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
  - Plyometrics: 4 to 10 reps per set, ground contact under 0.2 s, for example low depth jumps from a 20 to 30 cm box, skipping, hopping and bounding. Foot contacts rose from about 30 to 60 per session in week one to 110 to 228 after 6 to 9 weeks. In moderately trained runners, plyometrics improved economy more than explosive lifting. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Dose. Most successful runner studies used 2 to 3 supervised sessions a week; two may be enough, since a review of 311 endurance runners found only a weak link between weekly frequency and economy gains. Changes showed up after about 6 weeks (some economy gains after 4). Rest between sets was usually 2 to 3 minutes. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] General adult guidance agrees on at least 2 sessions a week working all major muscle groups with high effort, 2 to 3 sets per exercise, with diminishing returns beyond that for strength. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026]
- Effort, failure and equipment. Training to failure adds no strength, size or power; ending sets about 2 to 3 reps short of failure is enough. Machines and free weights produced similar strength gains in general adults. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] Runner studies similarly found failure unnecessary. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Progression. Some lifting beats none, one set beats none and two beat one; progressive overload is needed for continued gains, and load matters more for strength than any other variable. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] Novice runners typically began with 1 to 2 sets and built to 3 to 6. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Fitting it around running. Most runner studies put strength on separate days or in a separate session on the same day; only three did it right before or after a run. A single lifting session can impair running the next day, hence at least 24 hours before a hard run and more than 3 hours after hard running on the same day. Lifting in place of running gave no extra benefit over lifting on top of running. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] In the women-only trial, strength was ideally on non-endurance days, and on double days it came first. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- Through a marathon block. Dropping to one session a week during the racing season maintained gains, and stopping altogether brought runners back to baseline within 6 weeks. There is not enough evidence for phase-by-phase or taper-specific advice. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Safety. Across more than 38,000 adults in exercise trials, training did not raise serious adverse events. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] Studies where lifting was unsupervised, or supervised only for the first 2 weeks, showed no strength gains, so a qualified coach matters for beginners. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]

## Where they differ
- Does lifting improve running economy in women? The pooled review (mostly men) found 2 to 8% gains. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] The women-only trial found no economy change and no change in 40-minute run performance despite a 40% rise in leg strength. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016] Collegiate women gained more than men on most lab tests and likely raced about 1.2% faster. [@barnes-resistance-training-cross-country-2013] Likely reasons: the women-only trial tested at a slow speed (10 km/h on a 5.3% incline), while the review saw gains mostly at faster speeds; its sample was small (19 finishers), and its athletes also cycled. The review also notes six other studies with no economy gain. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016] [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Heavy vs plyometric. Collegiate runners in season did better on several lab measures with heavy lifting than with plyometrics mixed with heavy lifting, and in women heavy-only was possibly better. [@barnes-resistance-training-cross-country-2013] The review found plyometrics beat explosive lifting for economy in moderately trained runners and favors a mix over time. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] The studies compared different things (heavy vs heavy plus plyometric, versus plyometric vs explosive), so they are not directly contradictory.
- Men vs women in season. Lifting during a racing season was possibly harmful to men's race times (about 0.5% slower, wide uncertainty) but likely helpful to women's; the authors advise caution for men in season. [@barnes-resistance-training-cross-country-2013] The review found racing-season maintenance at one session a week worked, without a sex split. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Load. General adult guidance ties maximal strength to loads of 80% of one-rep max or more, with a dose response. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] In runners, low-rep heavy lifting (3 to 5 reps at 80% or more) was not better than moderate loads (60 to 80%, 5 to 15 reps). [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] The difference is the goal: maximal strength in general adults versus running outcomes in runners.
- Failure. The women-only trial took sets to the target rep max with help on the last rep, and still found no endurance loss. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016] Both reviews say failure is unnecessary. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Periodization. General adult guidance found periodized programs no better than non-periodized ones when overload progressed. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] The runner review favors a mixed or periodized approach long term but could not give phase-specific advice. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Injury. The runner review's authors suggest heavy lifting may lower injury risk long term, but the trials measured performance, not injuries. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018] General adult guidance reports no rise in serious adverse events with training. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] No approved source tests strength work as running-injury prevention.

## Women-specific notes
- Only about 96 of 469 runners in the main review were women, with two women-only studies; the authors list female runners as under-researched. Treat its economy numbers as mostly male evidence. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Well-trained women (VO2max about 53 ml/kg/min; 19 finished: 11 strength plus endurance, 8 endurance only) did 2 sessions a week for 11 weeks of half squat, single-leg leg press, single-leg hip flexion and calf raises, 3 sets, progressing from 10RM/6RM to 6RM/4RM. Leg strength rose about 40%, jumps 6 to 9%, and muscle fibers grew (type II about 31%), with no loss of endurance or capillaries. Running economy and 40-minute run performance did not change. The study did not report menstrual cycle phase or contraception. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- In 50 collegiate cross-country runners (men and women) lifting during a 13-week season, women gained more than men on most lab tests, and lab gains matched race gains for women. Women likely raced about 1.2% faster, and heavy lifting alone was possibly better than heavy plus plyometric. The authors suggest women include heavy lifting. [@barnes-resistance-training-cross-country-2013]
- Combining strength and endurance training blunted lower-body strength gains in men but not in women; relative strength gains are otherwise similar between the sexes. See cross-training.md for detail. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024]
- The 2026 general adult guidance gives no sex-specific recommendations. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026]

## Gaps
- No approved source tests strength training in marathon runners or for marathon performance; trials used 1.5 to 10 km time trials or a 40-minute run.
- No approved source tests strength or plyometric work for preventing running injuries (bone stress, tendon problems). A candidate source is suggested in the audit report.
- No phase-by-phase periodization or taper guidance for strength in a marathon block beyond in-season maintenance.
- Core, hip and upper-body work for runners is not covered by any approved source.
- Menstrual cycle phase and contraception were not reported in the women-tested strength studies.
- Barnes 2013 was read from its abstract only (copyrighted, not fetchable); exact sex counts and program details were not checked.
