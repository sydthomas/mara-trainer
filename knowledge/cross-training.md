# Cross-training and concurrent training

## Bottom line for the coach
- The approved sources that touch cross-training (national activity guidelines and a published plan index) give no comparison of cycling, swimming, the elliptical or pool running with running for marathon fitness, and nothing on cross-training while injured. Say so plainly instead of giving numbers, and send injury questions to a qualified professional. [@us-physical-activity-guidelines-2nd-ed] [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- Published beginner marathon plans do use cross-training: one novice plan pairs it with rest days, and a three-runs-a-week plan uses slightly more of it to fill the week. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- The general health floor for adults is at least 150 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, or at least 75 minutes of vigorous activity such as running, plus muscle-strengthening on at least 2 days a week. A marathon runner is well past the aerobic floor; the strength part still applies. [@us-physical-activity-guidelines-2nd-ed]
- Combining strength and endurance training can cause a small to moderate "interference" with strength gains, but in pooled data it blunted lower-body strength gains in men and not in women. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024]
- For trained endurance athletes, adding strength work did not blunt VO2max; only untrained people saw a small blunting. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024] In runners, strength work did not lower VO2max. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- To limit interference, keep lifting at least 24 hours before a hard run and more than 3 hours after hard running on the same day. For lifting doses and exercise choice, see strength.md. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]

## What the sources agree on
- Health baseline. Adults should move more and sit less; some activity is better than none. Key adult targets are 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity (or at least 75 minutes of vigorous activity like running) and muscle-strengthening on at least 2 days a week. Short bouts count; there is no minimum bout length in the 2018 edition. [@us-physical-activity-guidelines-2nd-ed]
- Concurrent training, pooled. Across 59 studies (1,346 healthy adults, mostly untrained in strength, average age about 27), training strength and endurance together slightly blunted lower-body strength gains versus strength alone in men (SMD about -0.43) but not in women (about +0.08); the sex difference was significant. There was no sex difference for upper-body strength or VO2max. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024]
- Training status matters for endurance. Concurrent training blunted VO2max in endurance-untrained people (SMD about -0.35) but not in trained or highly trained endurance athletes. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024] The runner review likewise found VO2max mostly unchanged when strength work was added to running. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Well-trained women who both ran and cycled (19 finishers) added two heavy leg sessions a week for 11 weeks to about 5.8 hours of endurance training and kept their VO2max, lactate threshold speed and capillary density, while getting about 40% stronger. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- Scheduling to limit interference. Most runner studies did strength on separate days or as a separate session on the same day, since one lifting session can impair a run the following day; the review advises at least 24 hours between lifting and a hard run and more than 3 hours after hard running before lifting. Replacing running with strength work gave no extra benefit over adding it. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]
- Cross-training in published plans. One widely used beginner plan pairs cross-training with plenty of rest to avoid overtraining, and a three-run-days plan adds slightly more cross-training than the others. The page does not say which activities. [@hal-higdon-marathon-training]
- Strength-only sources. The 2026 position stand covers lifting only; its evidence on combining lifting with aerobic training was too limited to draw conclusions. Use strength.md for its content. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026]

## Where they differ
- Is there interference at all? A 2024 meta-analysis found small to moderate interference (lower-body strength in men, VO2max in the untrained, and power, clearly in women and borderline in men). [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024] The 2026 strength position stand judged the concurrent-training evidence insufficient to draw conclusions. [@acsm-resistance-training-2026] They differ in method: one is a sex- and training-status meta-analysis built for this question, the other an overview of reviews on resistance training in which concurrent training was a side topic.
- Power in women. Lower-body strength was not blunted in women, but power was (SMD about -0.52, versus about -0.27 in men, no significant sex difference). [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024] In the women-only trial, jump height still rose 6 to 9% with heavy lifting added to endurance work, though there was no strength-only group to compare. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- Does the endurance side suffer? The meta-analysis says endurance outcomes may be blunted, but only in untrained people. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024] Runner studies, mostly in trained runners, found no VO2max loss, though they had no strength-only group, so strength interference could not be tested there. [@blagrove-strength-training-distance-runners-2018]

## Women-specific notes
- Women showed no blunting of lower-body strength gains from concurrent training, while men did; the authors suggest men's higher share of fast-twitch fiber area and greater fatigability may explain it, but the mechanism is unknown. Hypertrophy data were men-only. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024]
- Fewer studies included women, menstrual cycle phase and pill use were rarely controlled, and low energy availability may complicate findings in highly trained women. [@huiberts-concurrent-training-sex-2024]
- The only women-only endurance-plus-strength trial among approved sources found no loss of endurance fitness with heavy lifting. [@vikmoen-heavy-strength-female-endurance-2016]
- The national guidelines say women who were vigorously active before pregnancy can keep going during pregnancy and postpartum while under a health care provider's care. Any pregnancy or postpartum question goes to her provider. [@us-physical-activity-guidelines-2nd-ed]

## Gaps
- No approved source on cycling, swimming, elliptical or deep-water running for runners: how fitness transfers between them, how to count them toward weekly load, or how much they can replace running.
- No approved source on cross-training during injury or return to running (apart from postnatal guidance in the registry); these questions go to a qualified professional.
- Moderators of interference (running vs cycling, same session vs separate days, session order, frequency) are in the concurrent-training meta-analysis tables, which were not read.
- The U.S. guidelines' full adult section (including extra-benefit thresholds above 300 minutes) was only partly readable from the executive summary PDF; only the targets above were confirmed.
- Hal Higdon plans were read from the public index page only; the plan tables were not read.
